Binomial distribution

Probability distribution of the number of successes in a sequence of independent events
For example, the number of heads in a sequence of coin flips

Described by n and p
n: total number of events
p: probability of success

Expected value
Expected value = n × p
Expected number of heads out of 10 flips = 10 × 0.5 = 5
If we don't know p, but know n and the expected value:
p = expected value / n

Independence
The binomial distribution is a probability distribution of the number of successes in a
sequence of independent events
Note: If events are not independent, the binomialdistribution does not apply!

General applications
The binomial distribution can be used for independent events producing binary outcomes
Clinical trial measuring drug effectiveness: Effective or not
Betting on the result of a sports match: Bettor can win or lose



📊 Distribución normal (campana de Gauss)
- Forma: simétrica, en forma de campana.
- Media (μ): punto central de la curva; también es la mediana y la moda.
- Desviación estándar (σ): indica qué tan dispersos están los datos respecto a la media.
- Regla empírica:
- μ ± 1σ → contiene aproximadamente el 68% de los datos.
- μ ± 2σ → contiene aproximadamente el 95% de los datos.
- μ ± 3σ → contiene aproximadamente el 99.7% de los datos.
Cada línea vertical representa:
- μ → centro de la distribución.
- μ ± 1σ → límites donde se concentra la mayoría de los datos.
- μ ± 2σ → límites donde se encuentran casi todos los datos.
- μ ± 3σ → extremos de la distribución, donde hay muy pocos datos.

The normal distribution, often visualized as a bell curve, is characterized by its symmetry and the property that the total area under the curve equals one. Key points include:

The distribution is defined by its mean and standard deviation, influencing its center and spread.
The 68-95-99.7 rule, which states that 68% of the data falls within one standard deviation of the mean, 95% within two, and 99.7% within three.
Real-world data, like exam scores, often closely follows a normal distribution, making it crucial for hypothesis testing.

📐 Kurtosis (forma del pico de la curva)
- Kurtosis normal (mesocúrtica): forma estándar de campana.
- Kurtosis positiva (leptocúrtica): pico más alto y colas más largas → más valores extremos.
- Kurtosis negativa (platicúrtica): curva más plana → menos valores extremos.

You also explored skewness and kurtosis as methods to describe the shape of distributions:

Skewness indicates the direction of the tail (positive/right or negative/left).
Kurtosis describes the peak's sharpness and the tail's fatness, with distributions categorized as leptokurtic (positive kurtosis), mesokurtic (normal distribution), or platykurtic (negative kurtosis).



Central limit theorem
The sampling distribution of a statistic becomes closer to the normal distribution as the size of
the sample increases.
* Samples should be random and independent



The Poisson distribution

Poisson processes
Average # of events in a period is known, but the time or space between events is random.

Examples:
Number of animals adopted from an animal shelter per week,
Number of people arriving at a restaurant per hour,
Number of website visits in a day.

Probability of some # of events occurring over a fixed period of time
Examples
Probability of at least five animals adopted from an animal shelter per week
Probability of 12 people arriving at a restaurant per hour
Probability of less than 200 visits to a website in a day
