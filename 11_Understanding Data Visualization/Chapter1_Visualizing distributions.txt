Continuous vs. categorical variables
In order to choose an appropriate type of plot to draw, you need to be able to distinguish between continuous variables (roughly: "things you can do arithmetic on") and categorical variables (roughly: "things that can be classified").

Continuos: Mass of squirrels, population of towns in Canada, Salary of employees.
Categorical: Provinces of towns in Canada, Job titles employees, whas the exam pass or failed ?


                                              Histograms
When should you use a histogram?
1. If you have a single continuous variable.
2. You want to ask questions about the shape of its distribution.

Modality of Histograms
How many peaks they have?
1- Unimodal, 2- Bimodal, 3- Trimodal.

Skewness of Histograms
Is it symmetric?
Left-skewed: graphic to te right
Right-skewed: graphic to the left
Symmetric: graphic in the middle.

Kurtosis
How many extreme values:
Mesokurtic: looks like bell-shape (normal distribution)
Leptokurtik: Narrow peak, and a lots of extreme values
Platykurtik: Broad peak, and few extreme values.



                                            Boxplots

Histogram Considerations
-First approach: Draw each histrogram in its own panel (graphic) and in a grid to better understand, it helps to compare against some graphics but not all
-Second approach: Stack all grhapics in a single columns, but graphic tunrs to small to read.
-Solution: Boxplot

When should you use a box plot?
1. When you have a continuous variable, split by a categorical variable.
2. When you want to compare the distributions of the continuous variable for each category.

You can compare many distribitions at once with Boxplots
